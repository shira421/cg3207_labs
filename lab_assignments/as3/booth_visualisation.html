How the hardware decides

Q₀ Q₋₁Accumulator operation
00Hold A
01A ← A + M
10A ← A − M
11Hold A

After the operation, shift {A, Q, Q₋₁} right by one bit, copying the sign bit into the leftmost position.

A and M have an extra bit. This makes −M representable even when M is the minimum negative input.

Why does Booth’s recoding work?

A run of ones can be written as a difference of powers of two: 001110 = 010000 − 000010. Booth’s inspects transitions between adjacent bits to choose subtraction at one boundary and addition at the other. Arithmetic shifting keeps the signed partial result aligned.

The template scans a sign-extended multiplier over 2n bits. This Booth implementation scans the original n-bit multiplier in n iterations. It is not a blanket claim that Booth always halves every multiplier’s latency.

What happens at one clock edge

1 · Read decision bitsInspect Q₀ and the saved previous bit Q₋₁.
2 · Form next AAdd M, subtract M, or keep A unchanged.
3 · Shift and registerArithmetic-shift the combined value and store A, Q and Q₋₁ together.

The RTL combines the operation and shift before one register update. They are visual substeps, not two separate clock cycles. The initial prepared values below are not an extra setup edge.
